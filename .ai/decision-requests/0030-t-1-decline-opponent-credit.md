# Decision Request: T-1 opponent credit on a decline (G3)

## Status

Blocked pending owner decision.

## Trigger

R8 says whether the opponent is credited "follows the ruleset shape"; the manual says nothing about crediting the other side.

## Evidence

- Manual p.67 says only that declining "will cost victory points"; it is silent on the opponent's score.
- Manual p.77: "Battles and other events will gain victory points." Not specific to declines.
- Research note: `docs/research/t-1-battle-offer-values.md` (provenance table, verbatim quotations).
- Spec #109 (R2), ticket #110; map #96 and decision tickets #97-#108.
- Prior art: ADR-0210, ADR-0213, ADR-0223, ADR-0226, ADR-0239; decision requests 0003, 0006, 0007, 0012.

## Why agent judgment is insufficient

Crediting the opponent changes both sides' war scores and so surrender and peace outcomes. Provenance class: UNSOURCED. The manual is silent, ADR-0210 forbids inventing values as fact, and spec #109 (R2, G1-G7) returns each such value to the owner individually. This is a gameplay balance number, not a routine local design choice.

## Options

### Option A: Owner supplies the value

- Behavior: the owner provides one of: no credit, mirror credit, or a fraction of the decline VP in basis points (0 to 10000) with provenance (a manual page, another high-trust source, or a stated design intent).
- Advantages: authoritative and unambiguous.
- Risks: the owner may not hold the original data; the value may not exist in the source game.
- Compatibility impact: none until authored.
- Migration impact: none (no prior state).
- Scope and effort: authoring in ticket #118 plus an ADR if the choice diverges from RTW3.

### Option B: Keep the value dormant

- Behavior: the ruleset section stays absent (or, if partially authored, the compile rejects it) so no production battle-offer behaviour depends on this value; Declining only reduces the decliner's score.
- Advantages: no unsourced value ships; the mechanics stay complete and tested against fixture-class values.
- Risks: the offer flow has no production effect; #118 and #119 stay blocked.
- Compatibility impact: none.
- Migration impact: none.
- Scope and effort: none.

### Option C: Admit as ADR-0213 `balance_adjustment`

- Behavior: the owner explicitly waives RTW3 fidelity for this value and admits an owner-chosen value as a labelled `balance_adjustment` ruleset constant with a recorded rationale (pattern of ADR-0223 and ADR-0239), never classed as manual. The owner chooses the credit shape and fraction.
- Advantages: unblocks #118 while staying honest about provenance.
- Risks: a deliberate divergence from the manual; must be tuned by the balance sweep.
- Compatibility impact: none until authored; authoring moves production behaviour and goldens once, at go-live.
- Migration impact: one golden re-pin at go-live per the golden repinning procedure.
- Scope and effort: an ADR plus authoring in #118.

## Recommendation

Option B is closest to the manual's wording, which names only a cost to the decliner; but the owner decides. Choosing none is still a value (a shape), so it needs an answer.

Minimum owner response: Choose none, mirror, or a fraction with its bp value.

## Work safely completed while blocked

The dormant mechanics (tickets #111 to #117) are specified and tested against labelled fixture-class values that never reach the production pack. Nothing is authored in production; #118 stays blocked on this decision.
