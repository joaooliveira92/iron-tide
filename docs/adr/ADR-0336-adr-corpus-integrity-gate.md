# ADR-0336: Every current ADR states its `Rule:`, holds one area, and supersedes acyclically

Applies to: repository

## Status

Superseded by: ADR-0366

Accepted (2026-10-29).

## Context

`docs/adr/by-scope/*.md` is the ADR context an agent reads before its package's records (ADR-0318). Each row is the
record's `Rule:` line, falling back to its title. Only 31 of 131 current records carried a `Rule:`, so 100 rows named a
change rather than the rule it leaves in force ("`@bluewave/events` moves to layer 4" states an event, not a constraint),
and the fallback made the compact view unreliable exactly where it was meant to save context.

`scripts/adr-index.cjs` documents that every ADR is assigned to exactly one area, but its partition check tested
presence only. ADR-0278 was listed under both "Process & documentation" and "Turns, interruptions & save/resume" and the
committed index rendered it twice without failing.

`validateRecords` proved that every supersession link exists, is reciprocated and agrees with `Status`, but a cycle
(A supersedes B, B supersedes A, both `superseded`, both reciprocating) satisfied all three while resolving to no
authority at all.

## Decision

- A current record — active, not superseded — must carry a non-empty `Rule:` line: one sentence stating what is
  authoritative now, citing no successor. Superseded records still carry none. A record that cannot be rendered
  compactly, or whose authority cannot be resolved unambiguously, is not authority an agent may follow.
- `scripts/adr-rule.baseline.json` lists the numbers of current records that predate this rule. The list only shrinks: a
  current record missing a `Rule:` fails unless baselined, and a baselined record that gains one fails until its entry is
  deleted.
- The area map is single-valued. A number assigned to two areas fails `pnpm adr:index`, naming both areas. Area stays a
  human navigation heading; `Applies to:` stays the machine-loaded scope.
- A supersession cycle over `Superseded by` edges fails the gate, naming the cycle.
- A higher ADR number never overrides a lower one. Authority moves only through `Supersedes:` or `Amends:`, and a
  reader takes the resolved current record, not the newest number.

## Consequences

- The generated by-scope pages state a rule on every current row, so an agent can act on the page without opening the
  record, and opening a record is for rationale rather than for authority.
- Writing a new ADR now requires one reviewable sentence beyond the title; the title may keep describing the change.
- The 100 grandfathered records are visible debt with a fixed ceiling, not an exemption. Each gains a `Rule:` as it is
  touched, and the baseline entry goes with it.
- Area placement is no longer a judgement for records that legitimately touch two areas: the record goes where a reader
  would look for it and `Applies to:` carries the rest.
